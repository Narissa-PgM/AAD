03 Operating model · proposed

Automation removes the repetitive work. People make the calls.

How I would run stewardship reporting at AA&D, in six parts. Choose a part, then select any step, stage or letter for detail.

1One Harvard · automation with guardrails

Automation takes the repetitive work. People keep every decision.

One Harvard means coordinating without formal authority: offices share the automation and the standards, and each office keeps its own decisions.

Read-only to systems of recordAutomation never changes a gift, fund or constituent record.
Every figure shows its sourceAmounts link to the record and the ledger they came from.
A named person approves every releaseNothing reaches a donor on a timer.
Owners keep their decisionsFinance, gift officers and academic units decide in their own areas.
Consent before storiesNo name, story or image without permission for that use.
Exceptions are loggedEvery variance, hold and override has a reason and a name.

How far automation goes

Level 0ManualPeople pull, match and draft by handStarting point
Level 1Automation assemblesPulls and ledger matches run on scheduleFirst cycle
Level 2Automation drafts, people approveDrafts from templates, a named approver signsCeiling for donor reports
Level 3Automation sendsReports go out without a personNot proposed

2AI drafting · human review and approval

AI drafts the letter. A person reviews, edits and signs it.

Adapted from my data-quality capstone: AI does the repetitive drafting from approved facts, and a named person makes every decision that reaches a donor.

Reviewer checklist

  • Fund name matches the gift agreementPulled from the gift record
  • Amount reconciled to the General LedgerFinance tie-out attached
  • Student story: consent pendingHOLD · story removed or consent obtained
  • Tone matches the house styleSteward edits are logged

Nothing sends without a named approver. AI never invents figures: it may only use facts that carry a source tag.

3Impact Circle · donor at the center

A circle has no last page. Every lap supports a bigger impact.

Working with FAS Development and partner offices, each report opens the next conversation, so support keeps growing instead of ending when the report is sent.

Why a circle

  • Thank and account. The first report shows the gift did what was promised.
  • Show the outcome. The next shows students, faculty and research it supported.
  • Invite deeper. Trust built over laps supports renewal, a new purpose or a larger gift.
