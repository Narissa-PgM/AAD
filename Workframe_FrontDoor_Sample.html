How the four programs move work

Standards down. Certified reports across. Questions back up.

This is the operating logic, not an org chart. Strategy sets the standard; donor and campaign turn it into products; team operations staffs the work and holds the release gate.

1 · Strategic leadershipSets priorities, tiers, standards and the questions the other programs answer.
2 · Donor impact reportingTurns the standard into a donor record, evidence set and report.
2 · Campaign & institutional initiativesTurns the priority into a board, public or school-facing product.
3 · Team leadership & operationsStaffs both production lines, keeps the calendar and holds the six-check release gate.

Shared by every program

One contract, four kinds of discipline

The four frameworks are not interchangeable. One is a working principle, one is a goal test, one is a control, and one is a donor narrative.

Principle

One Harvard

Schools, gift officers, Finance, Communications and data owners agree the work. The role coordinates; it does not claim authority over their systems.

Test

SMARTER

Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound, evaluable and reviewable. The test is whether the work can be checked, not whether the acronym is displayed.

Control

Release Gate

Intent, reconciliation, consent, traceability, attribution and authorization. A project status does not release a report; the gate does.

Narrative

VERITAS

A donor-narrative mnemonic built from the letters of Veritas: vision, experience, results, investment, truth, advancement, stewardship. The University motto itself means truth.

The Release Gate here is the publication authorization. Each platform's six QA gates are production checks; the platform pages are the source of truth for their own QA sequence.
